Mochi's food journey

Feeding a small menace isn't always simple. Here's what we went through, what finally worked, and answers to the questions dog moms ask us most.

We're dog parents, not vets. This page shares our own experience and isn't veterinary advice. Check with your vet before changing your dog's diet, and call them straight away if something seems wrong.

What we went through

  1. The struggle

    Skipped meals, an upset tummy every time we changed food, and itchy paws we couldn't explain. Every bag we bought seemed to make something worse.

  2. What we tried

    A vet visit to rule out anything medical, a food diary, fewer treats, and one change at a time so we could actually tell what was helping.

  3. What worked

    A food our vet recommended, introduced slowly over about ten days, fixed meal times and a strict treat budget. Mochi now cleans her bowl, and her tummy is calm.

Questions dog moms ask

How do I switch my dog to a new food without an upset stomach?

Slowly. We mixed the new food into the old one over about 7 to 10 days: roughly a quarter new for the first few days, then half and half, then three-quarters new, then all new. When Mochi's tummy complained, we stayed on the previous step for a couple more days.

Vomiting or diarrhea that keeps going is a reason to call the vet, not to wait it out.

My dog is a picky eater. What actually helped?

First, our vet checked for teeth or tummy problems, because sudden pickiness can be a sign that something hurts. After that, routine did more than any topper: meals at the same times, the bowl down for about 15 to 20 minutes, then picked up until the next meal.

We also cut back on treats, which are easy to overdo. A common rule of thumb is to keep treats under about 10% of your dog's daily calories.

How did you find out if it was a food allergy?

Itchy skin, paw licking and ear trouble can come from food, but also from fleas or things in the environment, so our vet helped us rule those out first.

The reliable way to test for a food allergy is an elimination diet set up by your vet, usually a new or specially processed protein for around 8 weeks or more, with nothing else on the side. At-home allergy test kits aren't considered reliable for food allergies.

Which human foods are dangerous for dogs?

The ones we keep well away from Mochi: chocolate, grapes and raisins, onions, garlic and chives, anything containing xylitol (sugar-free gum, some peanut butters and baked goods), macadamia nuts, alcohol, caffeine and cooked bones.

If your dog eats any of these, call your vet or an emergency animal clinic right away. Don't wait for symptoms.

Is grain-free food healthier?

Not automatically. True grain allergies are less common than the marketing suggests, and the U.S. FDA has investigated a possible link between some grain-free diets and a heart condition called dilated cardiomyopathy (DCM). We chose Mochi's food with our vet based on her needs, not on label trends.

How do I know if my dog is a healthy weight?

A quick check at home: you should be able to feel the ribs easily under a thin layer of padding, see a waist when you look down from above, and see the belly tuck up from the side.

The feeding guide on the bag is a starting point, not a rule. We adjusted Mochi's portions with our vet as her activity changed.
