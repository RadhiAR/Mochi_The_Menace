We're dog parents, not vets. These answers share what we've learned and what trusted vet guidelines say, but they aren't veterinary advice. Every dog is different, so check with your vet before making changes, and call them straight away if something seems wrong.

Food & diet

How much should I feed my dog?

Start with the feeding guide on your dog's food, using your dog's ideal weight rather than their current weight, then adjust based on body shape and activity. Most adult dogs do well on two meals a day; puppies usually need three or four smaller meals.

Treats count too: keep them under about 10% of your dog's daily calories. Your vet can tell you what your dog's ideal weight is.

How do I switch my dog to a new food without an upset stomach?

Slowly. Mix the new food into the old one over about 7 to 10 days: roughly a quarter new for the first few days, then half and half, then three-quarters new, then all new. If your dog's tummy complains, stay on the previous step for a couple more days.

Vomiting or diarrhea that keeps going is a reason to call the vet, not to wait it out.

Which human foods are dangerous for dogs?

Keep these well away from your dog: chocolate, grapes and raisins, onions, garlic, chives and leeks, macadamia nuts, alcohol, caffeine, raw bread dough, cooked bones, and anything containing xylitol, a sweetener also sold as "birch sugar" (sugar-free gum, some peanut butters, candy, baked goods and some medicines).

If your dog eats any of these, call your vet or an emergency animal clinic right away, even if they seem fine.

Is peanut butter safe for dogs?

Usually yes, in small amounts, as long as it has no xylitol, which can also be listed as birch sugar or wood sugar. Plain, unsalted peanut butter is the best choice. Check the label every time, especially on "sugar-free", "low sugar" and protein spreads.

Which fruits and vegetables can dogs eat?

Good small treats include plain cooked pumpkin (not pie filling), carrots, green beans, cucumber, blueberries, banana, apple slices without the seeds or core, and seedless watermelon without the rind.

Introduce one new food at a time, in small pieces. Skip grapes, raisins, onions, garlic and avocado.

Is grain-free food healthier?

Not automatically. True grain allergies are less common than the marketing suggests, and the U.S. FDA has investigated a possible link between some grain-free diets and a heart condition called dilated cardiomyopathy (DCM). Choose your dog's food with your vet based on your dog's needs, not label trends.

My dog is a picky eater. What helps?

First, have your vet check for teeth or tummy problems, because sudden pickiness can be a sign that something hurts. After that, routine does more than any topper: meals at the same times, the bowl down for about 15 to 20 minutes, then picked up until the next meal. Cutting back on treats helps a lot too.

How do I know if my dog has a food allergy?

Itchy skin, paw licking and ear trouble can come from food, but also from fleas or things in the environment, so your vet will usually rule those out first.

The reliable way to test for a food allergy is an elimination diet set up by your vet: usually a new or specially processed protein for around 8 weeks or more, with nothing else on the side. At-home allergy test kits aren't considered reliable for food allergies.

Why does my dog eat grass?

It's very common and usually not a sign of illness; many dogs simply like it. Just make sure the grass hasn't been treated with pesticides or fertilizer. If your dog eats grass frantically, vomits often or seems unwell, check in with your vet.

Health & vet care

Go to a vet or emergency clinic right away if your dog:

  • has trouble breathing, collapses or can't stand
  • has a swollen, hard belly or keeps trying to vomit but nothing comes up
  • vomits or has diarrhea again and again, or there's blood
  • has eaten something toxic, or has a seizure
  • has pale, blue or very red gums
  • is straining but can't pee
  • has been hit by a car or badly hurt, or shows signs of heatstroke
How often should my dog see the vet?

Healthy adult dogs should have a checkup once a year. Puppies need several visits in their first months for their vaccine series. Senior dogs (around age 7 and up, earlier for giant breeds) benefit from checkups every six months, because problems are easier to treat when they're caught early.

Which vaccines does my dog need?

North American vet guidelines (from AAHA) recommend "core" vaccines for every dog: distemper, parvovirus, adenovirus (hepatitis), rabies and, since 2024, leptospirosis.

Others, like Bordetella (kennel cough), Lyme disease and canine influenza, depend on your dog's lifestyle and where you live. Rabies vaccination is also required by law in many places. Your vet will set the right schedule for your dog.

Does my dog need flea, tick and heartworm prevention all year?

Ask your vet what fits where you live, but many vets recommend year-round protection. Blacklegged ticks, which can carry Lyme disease, become active whenever it's above about 4°C, so mild winter days count too. Heartworm spreads through mosquito bites.

After walks in grass or woods, run your fingers through your dog's coat to check for ticks, especially around the ears, toes and armpits. We check Mochi after every park trip.

When should I spay or neuter my dog?

It depends on your dog's adult size. For small breeds (under about 20 kg or 45 lb as adults), AAHA guidelines suggest neutering males at around 6 months and spaying females before their first heat, at around 5 to 6 months.

For large breeds, waiting until they stop growing (roughly 9 to 15 months) may lower the risk of some joint problems and cancers. Talk through the timing with your vet.

How can I tell if my dog is in pain? Can I give human pain medicine?

Signs of pain include limping, not wanting to jump or use stairs, hiding, panting or restlessness while resting, licking one spot, eating less, or growling when touched.

Never give human pain relievers like ibuprofen, naproxen or acetaminophen (Tylenol) unless your vet tells you to: they can seriously harm dogs. Your vet can prescribe dog-safe pain relief.

Why is my dog scooting on the floor?

Usually because of full or irritated anal glands, two small scent glands near the bottom. Worms, allergies or skin irritation can also cause it. If it keeps happening, your vet or groomer can check and empty the glands.

How do I take care of my dog's teeth?

Brushing is the gold standard: daily is best, and a few times a week still helps. Use toothpaste made for dogs, never human toothpaste, which can contain xylitol or fluoride. Dental chews with the VOHC seal can help between brushings, and many dogs need a professional cleaning from time to time.

Bad breath, red gums or dropping food while eating can mean dental pain.

Does a dry, warm nose mean my dog is sick?

No, that's a myth. A dog's nose changes from wet to dry through the day, after naps or in dry air. Look at the bigger picture instead: energy, appetite, drinking and bathroom habits.

Grooming & beauty care

How often should I bathe my dog?

For most dogs, every 4 to 6 weeks, or whenever they're dirty or smelly. Bathing too often can dry out the skin. Use a shampoo made for dogs, because human shampoo is made for different skin and can irritate it. Rinse well and dry thoroughly, especially in winter.

How often should I trim my dog's nails?

Usually every 3 to 4 weeks. A good sign it's time: you can hear clicking on hard floors. Trim a little at a time to avoid the quick, the pink part with blood vessels inside. Keep styptic powder handy in case of a nick, or try a nail grinder.

If nail trims are stressful, groomers and vet clinics can do them quickly.

How often should I brush my dog?

It depends on the coat. Short, smooth coats need brushing about once a week. Long or double coats need it several times a week, and daily during shedding season. Wiry terrier coats, like some Jack Russells have, may need hand-stripping by a groomer to stay neat.

Brushing also spreads natural oils and helps you spot lumps, ticks and skin problems early.

How do I clean my dog's ears?

Use a vet-approved ear cleaner: fill the ear canal, gently massage the base of the ear, let your dog shake, then wipe the outer ear with cotton or gauze. Never push cotton swabs deep into the ear.

A bad smell, redness, discharge, head shaking or lots of scratching can mean an ear infection, which needs a vet.

Should I shave my dog in summer?

Usually not if your dog has a double coat, like huskies, golden retrievers or Pomeranians. That coat helps protect against heat and sunburn, and shaving can change how it grows back. Brushing out the loose undercoat keeps them cooler.

Dogs with single coats that are normally clipped, like poodles, can have shorter summer cuts.

Do dogs need sunscreen?

Dogs with thin or light fur, or pink noses and ears, can get sunburned. Use a sunscreen made for dogs, because some human sunscreens contain zinc oxide, which is harmful if licked. Shade and avoiding the midday sun help the most.

Why does my dog still smell after a bath?

A smell that lingers often comes from the ears, teeth, anal glands or a skin infection, or from a coat that stayed damp. If the smell comes back quickly after baths, a vet check is worth it.

Training & behavior

How much exercise does my dog need?

It depends on breed, age and health, but many adult dogs need somewhere between 30 minutes and 2 hours of activity a day. High-energy breeds like Jack Russell terriers need plenty of running plus brain work: sniffing walks, puzzle toys and training games.

How do I stop my dog from pulling on the leash?

Stop walking whenever the leash gets tight, and move forward again when it loosens. Reward your dog with treats and praise for walking beside you. A front-clip harness can help while you train. Keep sessions short, and practice somewhere boring before trying busy streets.

My dog chases squirrels. How do I manage it?

Terriers were bred to hunt small animals, so chasing comes naturally. Keep your dog on a leash in unfenced areas, practice a strong recall and "leave it" with really good treats, and give the instinct a safe outlet with tug, flirt poles and sniffing games.

How do I help a shy or timid dog feel brave?

Go at your dog's pace. Let them watch new people and dogs from a comfortable distance, reward calm behavior, and let them choose when to say hello. Never force an interaction. Short, positive experiences build confidence over time.

If the fear is severe, a certified trainer or a veterinary behaviorist can help.

How do I help my dog with separation anxiety?

Practice short absences and build up time alone slowly. Exercise before you leave, offer a long-lasting chew or puzzle toy, and keep goodbyes and hellos calm. A pet camera can show how your dog really copes.

Destructive behavior, nonstop barking or accidents when alone are worth discussing with your vet or a behavior professional.

Products & gear

Harness or collar: which is better?

A harness spreads pressure across the chest instead of the neck, which is great for small dogs, pullers and dogs with breathing or neck issues. A collar is still handy for ID tags, so many dog parents use both. You should be able to fit two fingers under any strap.

Which toys and chews are safe?

Choose toys too big to swallow, supervise chewing, and throw away toys that are falling apart. Avoid cooked bones, and be careful with very hard chews like antlers: a common vet tip is that if you can't dent it with your fingernail, it may be hard enough to crack a tooth.

Should I microchip my dog?

Yes. It's one of the best ways to get a lost dog home. A vet places a tiny chip under the skin in a quick appointment. The chip only helps if your contact details are registered and up to date, so update them whenever you move, and keep an ID tag on the collar too.

Is pet insurance worth it?

It can be, especially for big unexpected bills like surgery or emergencies. Compare the monthly cost, deductible, reimbursement percentage and what's excluded. Pre-existing conditions usually aren't covered, so many people sign up while their dog is young and healthy. Some prefer a dedicated savings account instead. Either way, have a plan.

Does my dog need a winter coat or boots?

Small, short-coated or lean dogs feel the cold faster and often benefit from a coat. Boots protect paws from ice, snowballs between the toes and road salt. If your dog won't wear them, wipe their paws with a warm damp cloth after walks and use a paw balm.

Shivering, lifting paws or slowing down means it's time to head home. (A very Canadian dog question.)

Everyday life & rescue pups

How hot is too hot for a walk?

Press the back of your hand on the pavement for 7 seconds: if it's too hot for you, it's too hot for paws. On hot days, walk early or late, bring water and choose shady routes. Never leave a dog in a parked car, even with the windows cracked, because it heats up dangerously in minutes.

Heavy panting, drooling, bright red gums, wobbling or vomiting can mean heatstroke. Move your dog somewhere cool, wet them with cool (not ice-cold) water and get to a vet.

How long can I leave my dog home alone?

Many trainers suggest adult dogs shouldn't be left more than about 4 to 6 hours without a potty break and some company; puppies need breaks much more often. A dog walker, a midday visit or doggy daycare can help on long days.

How long does a rescue dog take to settle in?

Many rescues use the "3-3-3" guide: about 3 days to decompress (your dog may be nervous or not eat much), 3 weeks to learn your routine, and 3 months to truly feel at home. Every dog is different, so be patient, keep a steady routine and let trust grow.

Mochi is a rescue too, and she says it was worth the wait.

Is it okay for my dog to sleep in my bed?

It's a personal choice. For healthy dogs and people it's generally fine, and many dogs love it. Keep up flea and tick prevention, and if your dog guards the bed or you're losing sleep, a cozy dog bed next to yours works just as well.
